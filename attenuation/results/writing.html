Why is this question interesting / why did you choose it?

what belongs here
Two beats. The literature hook: models have internal representations of whether they recognise an entity, and those causally gate refusal — Do I Know This Entity?, Ferrando, Obeso, Rajamanoharan & Nanda, ICLR 2025 oral. That is self-knowledge about what the model learned. This asks the same about what it was told. Their result predicts a degraded fact should behave like an unknown entity and trigger a refusal.

Where it came from, two sentences, not a story: running V-Steer (COLM 2026) across ten models, the failures were quiet — an account number ending 02 when the user had said 302. Not a broken model; a model confidently misreading.

Technical setup — what do you quantify, and how do you define and measure it?

what belongs here
Models. Qwen3.5-4B first (his named default), Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 second. Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct failed its own control and is excluded.
Items. 100: 10 kinds of fact × 10 values, one clause in a fixed frame, values deliberately unguessable (Wobbet, QR318).
The knob. Subtract b from the attention logits at that sentence's token positions, before the softmax — the sentence's relative weight is multiplied by e^-b. At b=3 it keeps 5%, at 6 it keeps 0.25%. Greedy decoding throughout, so temperature is not a variable.
Conditions. present / faint / swap / drop.
The metric. The rate of answering "yes" to "Did I tell you X? Answer only yes or no." One-word answer to a direct instruction, not free-text classification.
Gate. An item counts only if the unmanipulated model answers correctly and some b removes the value. Both failure kinds counted.

What is the strongest evidence you found against these hypotheses?

what belongs here
You have three, and they are the best material in the application.
  • The forced prefix. The first design pinned the read position with "Your dog is called ___", which makes "I don't know" grammatically impossible. A forced completion was being read as the model's choice. Removing it inverted the result.
  • A claim of yours that the baseline retired. "It misquotes the user" looked like a finding — Qwen3-4B does it 14 times in 100 with nothing manipulated at all.
  • Six items removed after reading them. 04:36 → "4:36 PM" is a correct answer and a substring test called it damage. Headline 151/189 → 145/183.
Also honest: the probe is out — its null returned 1.0 on ties, at the embedding layer where both conditions are literally the same vector.

What are the biggest limitations? Could you have addressed them?

what belongs here
Constructed conversations · one manipulation family · two models after exclusion, both 4B · greedy, one seed · faint is a per-item threshold.

Items are not fully independent: Qwen3-4B gives only 55 distinct answers across 97 items — five account numbers produce the same refusal word for word.

And the one to state without apology: this is an idealised version of a state that arises in deployment for other reasons — KV cache compression and eviction, KV quantisation, long-context dilution, prompt compression. You measured the idealised version because the dose can be controlled. None of those is measured here.
